Hidden deep within the emerald Raghunandan hills of northern Tripura lies one of India's most mystical archaeological treasures: **Unakoti**, complemented by the nearby transit and plantation town of **Kumarghat**. Known as the "Angkor Wat of the Northeast," Unakoti is a mesmerizing open-air forest gallery of monumental rock-cut bas-reliefs, stone statues, and sacred natural waterfalls dating back to between the 7th and 9th centuries AD.

Contrasting the silent, ancient stone faces staring out from the misty rainforest with the sun-drenched pineapple hills and tea estates of Kumarghat, this northern circuit in Tripura offers travelers an unforgettable adventure off the beaten tourist path.

## How to Reach Unakoti and Kumarghat

By Air: The closest airport is Maharaja Bir Bikram Airport (IXA) in Agartala, located approximately 160 km away. Alternatively, Silchar Airport (IXS) in Assam is about 155 km to the northeast. Taxis and rental cars can be booked from either airport.
By Train: Kumarghat Railway Station (KUGT) is located just 22 km from the Unakoti heritage site, while Dharmanagar Railway Station (DMR) is roughly 18 km away. Both stations enjoy frequent train services connecting from Agartala, Silchar, Lumding, and Guwahati.
By Road: Unakoti is accessible via well-paved state highways branching off National Highway 8 (NH-8). State buses, shared jeeps, and private taxis run regularly from Agartala (around 4.5 to 5 hours drive), Kailashahar (8 km away), and Kumarghat.

## 2-Day Itinerary for Unakoti and Kumarghat

### Day 1: The Colossal Stone Deities of Unakoti

Arrive in Kumarghat or Kailashahar in the morning, check into your forest lodge or hotel, and head towards the **Unakoti Rock Carvings**.
Enter through the shaded rainforest gateway and descend the stone steps into the deep mountain ravine where a natural spring, known as *Kunda*, tumbles over mossy boulders.
Stand in awe before the centerpiece of Unakoti: **Unakotiswara Kal Bhairava**. This gigantic 30-foot-tall rock-cut face of Lord Shiva features stylized eyes, elaborate earrings, and a towering 10-foot embroidered headdress adorned with celestial warriors and floral motifs. Beside the face, a sacred mountain spring cascades directly across the rock face.
Venture across the hillside pathway to admire the exquisite relief of **Goddess Ganga** riding her mythical makara (crocodile), followed by two colossal carved figures of **Lord Ganesha**, one of which features three pairs of arms and a prominent tusk.
Spend the afternoon hiking along the winding trails across the gorge, discovering lesser-known stone sculptures of Vishnu, Ravana, and Nandi bull scattered amidst towering bamboo thickets.
Sit quietly by the natural stream as late afternoon sunlight filters through the canopy, illuminating the ancient stone carvings with golden hues, before returning to your base for dinner.

### Day 2: Kumarghat's Pineapple Orchards and Verdant Hills

Start your morning with a scenic drive to **Kumarghat**, renowned as the pineapple capital of Tripura.
Visit the lush government fruit orchards and private plantations cascading down rolling hill slopes. Learn how Tripura's sweet, low-acidity Queen and Kew pineapples are organically cultivated, harvested, and processed into canned slices and juices.
Next, drive through the verdant expanses of the **Fatikroy Tea Estates** bordering Kumarghat. Enjoy a refreshing walk through the manicured emerald tea bushes and observe local tea pickers at work.
If time permits, explore the fringes of the nearby **Rowa Wildlife Sanctuary**, a small protected forest tract home to numerous species of migratory birds, barking deer, wild boars, and playful primates.
Before bidding farewell, pick up fresh organic pineapples, jars of fruit preserves, and traditional woven cane baskets from the local Kumarghat bazaar.

## FAQs and Tips for Visiting Unakoti and Kumarghat

The best time to visit is from October to March when temperatures are mild and pleasant. Avoid the peak monsoon months (June to August), as heavy downpours can make stone steps in the gorge slippery and moss-covered.
Wear comfortable walking shoes with good grip. Exploring Unakoti requires climbing up and down hundreds of stone steps built into the hillside terrain.
Unakoti comes alive during the grand festival of **Ashokastami** (celebrated in March or April), when thousands of pilgrims congregate here to bathe in the holy stream and take part in colorful temple rituals.
Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) maintenance is actively underway to preserve the rock faces from rainwater erosion; please respect the site by sticking to marked paths and refraining from touching the carvings.
Basic tea stalls and eateries are available at the entrance of Unakoti, but for hearty sit-down meals, dining at Kailashahar or Kumarghat town is recommended.
